## Definition

In reinforcement learning, the **behavior policy** generates the agent's experience, while the **target policy** is the policy being evaluated or improved.

In **on-policy learning**, the behavior policy and target policy are the same. The agent therefore learns about the policy it currently follows.

In **off-policy learning**, the behavior policy and target policy differ. The agent can use experience generated by one policy to learn about another policy.

[[Sarsa]] is an on-policy algorithm because its update uses the next action selected by the current behavior policy.

[[Q-Learning]] is an off-policy algorithm because its update uses the greatest estimated action value in the next state, independently of the action actually selected by the behavior policy.

## Mathematical Formulation

Let the behavior policy be denoted by

$$
\pi_b(a\mid s)
$$

and the target policy by

$$
\pi_T(a\mid s).
$$

For on-policy learning, both policies are identical:

$$
\pi_b=\pi_T.
$$

For off-policy learning, they may differ:

$$
\pi_b\neq\pi_T.
$$

The on-policy character of Sarsa can be seen from its target:

$$
\bar{q}_t=r_{t+1}+\gamma q_t(s_{t+1},a_{t+1}),
$$

where the next action is sampled from the behavior policy:

$$
a_{t+1}\sim\pi_b(\cdot\mid s_{t+1}).
$$

The Q-learning target is instead

$$
\bar{q}_t=r_{t+1}+\gamma\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}(s_{t+1})}q_t(s_{t+1},a).
$$

The behavior policy may select an exploratory action, but the target assumes that a greedy action is selected in the next state. Therefore, the policy generating the experience can differ from the greedy target policy.

## Comparison

| Property | On-policy learning | Off-policy learning |
|---|---|---|
| Behavior policy | Generates the experience | Generates the experience |
| Target policy | Same as the behavior policy | May differ from the behavior policy |
| Exploration | Directly affects the learned policy | Can be separated from the target policy |
| Example | [[Sarsa]] | [[Q-Learning]] |

Off-policy learning makes it possible to learn from experience generated by an exploratory policy, another agent, a human, or a previously collected dataset.
