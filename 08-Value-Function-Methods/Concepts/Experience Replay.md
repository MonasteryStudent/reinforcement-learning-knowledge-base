## Definition

**Experience replay** is a technique in which experience samples are stored in a dataset called a **replay buffer** instead of being used only once in the order in which they were generated.

An experience sample contains a state, an action, an immediate reward, and the resulting next state:

$$
(s,a,r,s').
$$

During training, mini-batches of samples are drawn uniformly from the replay buffer. Consequently, the order in which the samples are used differs from the order in which they were collected.

Experience replay serves two main purposes:

- Random sampling reduces the temporal correlations between consecutive experience samples.
- Stored samples can be used multiple times, increasing data efficiency.

In [[Deep Q-Learning]], experience replay provides the mini-batches used to train the main network. Because Q-learning can learn from experience generated by a different behavior policy, it is suitable for this [[On-Policy and Off-Policy Learning|off-policy]] use of stored data.

## Mathematical Formulation

Let

$$
\mathcal{B}
=
\left\{
(s,a,r,s')
\right\}
$$

denote the replay buffer.

If the buffer contains $N$ experience samples and samples are drawn uniformly, every stored sample has the same probability of being selected:

$$
p\left((s,a,r,s')\right)
=
\frac{1}{N}.
$$

A mini-batch containing $m$ samples is denoted by

$$
\mathcal{M}
=
\left\{
(s_i,a_i,r_i,s_i')
\right\}_{i=1}^{m},
$$

where the samples are drawn uniformly from $\mathcal{B}$.

For each sample, the Deep Q-learning target is

$$
y_{T,i}
=
r_i
+
\gamma
\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}(s_i')}
\hat{q}(s_i',a,\mathbf{w}_T).
$$

The main network is trained on the mini-batch by minimizing

$$
L(\mathbf{w})
=
\frac{1}{m}
\sum_{i=1}^{m}
\left[
y_{T,i}
-
\hat{q}(s_i,a_i,\mathbf{w})
\right]^2.
$$

Uniform sampling applies to the stored experience samples. It does not require the samples to be processed in the sequence in which they were generated.

## Procedure

1. Generate an experience sample $(s,a,r,s')$ by interacting with the environment.
2. Store the sample in the replay buffer $\mathcal{B}$.
3. Uniformly draw a mini-batch $\mathcal{M}$ from the replay buffer.
4. Calculate a target value for every sample in the mini-batch.
5. Train the main network using the resulting target values.
6. Keep the stored samples available for later training iterations.

Through repeated random sampling, one experience sample may contribute to several parameter updates.
