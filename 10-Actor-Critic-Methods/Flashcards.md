## Card 10-001

**Front**

What is an actor-critic method?

**Back**

An actor-critic method combines:

- an **actor** that represents and updates a policy,
- a **critic** that estimates values and evaluates the actor's behavior.

Actor-critic methods combine policy-based and value-based learning.

## Card 10-002

**Front**

What are the roles of the actor and critic?

**Back**

The actor selects actions according to a parameterized policy

$$
\pi(a\mid s,\boldsymbol{\theta}).
$$

The critic estimates state or action values using parameters $\mathbf{w}$ and provides a learning signal, such as an action-value estimate or TD error, for the actor.

## Card 10-003

**Front**

What is the relationship between actor-critic methods and policy gradient methods?

**Back**

Actor-critic methods are policy gradient methods in which the values required by the policy update are estimated through temporal-difference learning.

REINFORCE instead estimates action values from complete Monte Carlo returns.

## Card 10-004

**Front**

What is Q Actor-Critic?

**Back**

Q Actor-Critic is the simplest actor-critic method.

Its actor represents a parameterized policy, while its critic approximates the action-value function

$$
q(s,a,\mathbf{w})
$$

using a one-step Sarsa update.

The actor uses the critic's action-value estimate to update the policy.

## Card 10-005

**Front**

What is a policy gradient baseline?

**Back**

A policy gradient baseline is a state-dependent scalar function

$$
b(s)
$$

that is subtracted from an action value in a policy-gradient update.

It does not change the expected policy gradient but can reduce the variance of its sample-based estimate.

10-Actor-Critic-Methods

## Card 10-006

**Front**

What is the advantage function?

**Back**

The advantage function measures the value of an action relative to the average action selected by the policy in the same state:

$$
\delta_\pi(s,a)
=
q_\pi(s,a)-v_\pi(s).
$$

A positive advantage means that the action is better than the policy's average action in that state.

10-Actor-Critic-Methods

## Card 10-007

**Front**

What is Advantage Actor-Critic?

**Back**

Advantage Actor-Critic, or A2C, uses a state-value critic and updates the actor according to an estimate of the advantage function.

The advantage is approximated by the one-step TD error:

$$
\delta_t
=
r_{t+1}
+
\gamma v(s_{t+1},\mathbf{w}_t)
-
v(s_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

It is also called TD actor-critic.

## Card 10-008

**Front**

What is importance sampling?

**Back**

Importance sampling estimates an expectation under a target distribution using samples generated by a different distribution.

Each sample is corrected by an importance weight:

$$
\frac{p_0(x)}{p_1(x)},
$$

where $p_0$ is the target distribution and $p_1$ is the sampling distribution.

## Card 10-009

**Front**

What is off-policy actor-critic?

**Back**

Off-policy actor-critic learns a target policy

$$
\pi(a\mid s,\boldsymbol{\theta})
$$

from experience generated by a different behavior policy

$$
\beta(a\mid s).
$$

Importance sampling corrects the difference between the action probabilities of the two policies.

## Card 10-010

**Front**

How does a deterministic policy differ from a stochastic policy?

**Back**

A stochastic policy produces a probability distribution over actions:

$$
\pi(a\mid s,\boldsymbol{\theta}).
$$

A deterministic policy directly maps every state to a single action:

$$
a
=
\mu(s,\boldsymbol{\theta}).
$$

## Card 10-011

**Front**

What is the basic idea of the deterministic policy gradient?

**Back**

The deterministic policy gradient changes the policy parameters so that the action produced by the policy has a greater action value.

It combines:

- how the parameters change the selected action,
- how a change in the action changes its action value.

## Card 10-012

**Front**

What is deterministic actor-critic?

**Back**

Deterministic actor-critic combines:

- a deterministic target policy $\mu(s,\boldsymbol{\theta})$,
- an action-value critic $q(s,a,\mathbf{w})$,
- an exploratory behavior policy $\beta$.

The actor directly produces actions, while the critic estimates their expected returns.

## Card 10-013

**Front**

How are the actor and critic updated in Q Actor-Critic?

**Back**

The actor update is

$$
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t+1}
=
\boldsymbol{\theta}_t
+
\alpha_\theta
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\ln\pi(a_t\mid s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)
q(s_t,a_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

The critic calculates

$$
\delta_t
=
r_{t+1}
+
\gamma q(s_{t+1},a_{t+1},\mathbf{w}_t)
-
q(s_t,a_t,\mathbf{w}_t)
$$

and updates

$$
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
=
\mathbf{w}_t
+
\alpha_w
\delta_t
\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}
q(s_t,a_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

## Card 10-014

**Front**

Why does subtracting a state-dependent baseline not change the expected policy gradient?

**Back**

For a fixed state $s$, the expected baseline contribution is

$$
\mathbb{E}_{A\sim\pi}
\left[
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\ln\pi(A\mid s,\boldsymbol{\theta})
b(s)
\right].
$$

This becomes

$$
b(s)
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\sum_a
\pi(a\mid s,\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
b(s)\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}1
=
0.
$$

Therefore, the baseline does not change the expected gradient.

## Card 10-015

**Front**

Why is the state value commonly used as a policy gradient baseline?

**Back**

Using

$$
b(s)=v_\pi(s)
$$

centers an action value around the policy's mean action value:

$$
q_\pi(s,a)-v_\pi(s).
$$

This produces the advantage function and can reduce the variance of the stochastic policy-gradient estimate without changing its expected value.

## Card 10-017

**Front**

How does importance sampling estimate an expectation under another distribution?

**Back**

An expectation under target distribution $p_0$ can be rewritten as

$$
\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_0}[X]
=
\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p_1}
\left[
\frac{p_0(X)}{p_1(X)}X
\right].
$$

The sampling distribution must satisfy

$$
p_1(x)>0
\quad
\text{whenever}
\quad
p_0(x)>0.
$$

Otherwise, some values possible under the target distribution could never be sampled.

## Card 10-018

**Front**

How is the importance weight used in off-policy actor-critic?

**Back**

For an action generated by behavior policy $\beta$, the importance weight is

$$
\frac{
\pi(a_t\mid s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)
}{
\beta(a_t\mid s_t)
}.
$$

It multiplies both the actor and critic updates:

$$
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t+1}
=
\boldsymbol{\theta}_t
+
\alpha_\theta
\frac{\pi(a_t\mid s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)}
{\beta(a_t\mid s_t)}
\delta_t
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\ln\pi(a_t\mid s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t),
$$

$$
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
=
\mathbf{w}_t
+
\alpha_w
\frac{\pi(a_t\mid s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)}
{\beta(a_t\mid s_t)}
\delta_t
\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}
v(s_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

## Card 10-019

**Front**

What is the deterministic policy gradient theorem?

**Back**

The deterministic policy gradient is

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}J(\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
\mathbb{E}_{S\sim\eta}
\left[
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\mu(S,\boldsymbol{\theta})
\nabla_a q_\mu(S,a)
\big|_{a=\mu(S,\boldsymbol{\theta})}
\right].
$$

The first gradient describes how the parameters change the action. The second describes how the action changes its expected return.

## Card 10-020

**Front**

How are the actor and critic updated in deterministic actor-critic?

**Back**

The critic calculates

$$
\delta_t
=
r_{t+1}
+
\gamma q\left(
s_{t+1},
\mu(s_{t+1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_t),
\mathbf{w}_t
\right)
-
q(s_t,a_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

The actor update is

$$
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t+1}
=
\boldsymbol{\theta}_t
+
\alpha_\theta
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\mu(s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)
\nabla_a q(s_t,a,\mathbf{w}_t)
\big|_{a=\mu(s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t)}.
$$

The critic update is

$$
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
=
\mathbf{w}_t
+
\alpha_w
\delta_t
\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}
q(s_t,a_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

The target action is generated by the deterministic target policy, while the action used to interact with the environment may come from a different exploratory behavior policy.
