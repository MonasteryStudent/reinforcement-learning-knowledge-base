## Definition

**Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C)** is an [[Actor-Critic Methods|actor-critic method]] that uses the [[Advantage Function]] to update the policy.

The actor represents a stochastic policy

$$
\pi(a\mid s,\boldsymbol{\theta}),
$$

while the critic approximates the [[State Value|state-value function]] as

$$
v(s,\mathbf{w}).
$$

The state value serves as a [[Policy Gradient Baseline]]. Instead of evaluating an action according to its absolute action value, the actor evaluates whether the outcome of the action was better or worse than expected in the current state.

Because the exact advantage is unknown, A2C approximates it using a one-step [[TD Target and TD Error|TD error]]. For this reason, the algorithm is also called **TD actor-critic**.

A2C is an [[On-Policy and Off-Policy Learning|on-policy]] algorithm because its experience samples are generated by the same policy that is evaluated and improved.

## Mathematical Formulation

Using the state value as a baseline, the policy gradient can be expressed as

$$
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}J(\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\ln\pi(A\mid S,\boldsymbol{\theta})
\left(
q_\pi(S,A)-v_\pi(S)
\right)
\right].
$$

The expression

$$
q_\pi(S,A)-v_\pi(S)
$$

is the advantage function.

For a sampled transition, the advantage is approximated by the one-step TD error:

$$
q_t(s_t,a_t)-v_t(s_t)
\approx
r_{t+1}
+
\gamma v(s_{t+1},\mathbf{w}_t)
-
v(s_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

The TD error is therefore

$$
\delta_t
=
r_{t+1}
+
\gamma v(s_{t+1},\mathbf{w}_t)
-
v(s_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

The actor uses the TD error to update the policy parameters:

$$
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t+1}
=
\boldsymbol{\theta}_t
+
\alpha_\theta
\delta_t
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\ln\pi(a_t\mid s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t).
$$

The critic uses the same TD error to update the state-value parameters:

$$
\mathbf{w}_{t+1}
=
\mathbf{w}_t
+
\alpha_w
\delta_t
\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}
v(s_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
$$

The TD error therefore has two roles:

- It approximates the advantage used by the actor.
- It provides the error used to train the critic.

Using the TD error requires only a state-value approximation. Separate approximations of both $q_\pi(s,a)$ and $v_\pi(s)$ are not required.

## Algorithm

Initialize the policy parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_0$, state-value parameters $\mathbf{w}_0$, and step sizes $\alpha_\theta,\alpha_w>0$.

At every time step in each episode:

1. Sample an action from the current policy:

   $$
   a_t
   \sim
   \pi(\cdot\mid s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t).
   $$

2. Execute $a_t$ and observe $r_{t+1}$ and $s_{t+1}$.
3. Calculate the TD error:

   $$
   \delta_t
   =
   r_{t+1}
   +
   \gamma v(s_{t+1},\mathbf{w}_t)
   -
   v(s_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
   $$

4. Update the actor:

   $$
   \boldsymbol{\theta}_{t+1}
   =
   \boldsymbol{\theta}_t
   +
   \alpha_\theta
   \delta_t
   \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
   \ln\pi(a_t\mid s_t,\boldsymbol{\theta}_t).
   $$

5. Update the critic:

   $$
   \mathbf{w}_{t+1}
   =
   \mathbf{w}_t
   +
   \alpha_w
   \delta_t
   \nabla_{\mathbf{w}}
   v(s_t,\mathbf{w}_t).
   $$

6. Continue with state $s_{t+1}$ until the episode terminates.

Because the policy is stochastic, it can generate exploratory actions directly without requiring an additional $\varepsilon$-greedy action-selection rule.
